Endnotes (1)

This entry appears in the same folio with the minutes of the meeting of 16 August 1507, written in a very different hand.

York prototype added note, Jalali Asheghabadi, 2024: The recurrent tensions between the carpenters and the cartwrights regarding the expenses associated with the Resurrection play, documented in various records including the 1500 House Books Y:B8, F101-101v and the 1507 House Books Y:B9, F37, merit further examination, particularly concerning how the play itself contributed to these disputes. Sheila K. Christie notes that the play features two distinct scenes—the court and the garden. In a 1998 Toronto production, as Christie observes, Pilate’s throne was set atop Jesus’ sarcophagus. This staging would have necessitated significant maintenance of the pageant wagon, likely placing considerable responsibility on the cartwrights. Both the 1500 and 1507 records highlight complaints from the cartwrights related to this issue. For more information, see: Christie, Sheila K., ‘Marking the Boundaries: Explorations of Meaning and Identity in the York Corpus Christi Cycle,’ PhD thesis (University of British Columbia, 2000), 29-41.
