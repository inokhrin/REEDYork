Source description
YCDHBHouse Books
Settlement: York
Repository: NYCRO
Shelfmark: B 5: 1483-9

Technical description: English, some Latin and Anglo-Norman in early volumes; paper (except B9, parchment); number of leaves varies from 41 (B12) to 402 (B32); smallest B30 (290mm x 180mm), largest B11 (395mm x 275/280mm), text in a single, wide, right-hand column, amount of leaf covered varies from scribe to scribe; gathered irregularly (the majority in 8s); modern foliation; little ornamentation in script until B29, ruled margins B29-B36, most marginal headings contemporary with the text; bound in white paper-covered boards resembling leather, shelf-mark gilt on red, spines of B30-36 more ornamental. All books damaged and repaired (damage most severe in early books, B1-B7 rectos badly faded, B2-4 missing first 3 leaves, last 2 gatherings B9 reversed, B20 missing ff 5-6, B28 opening leaves foliated A-U, some interleaving of letters and other single documets).

f 91v(27 June)

House Books Y:B2-4

[folio 91v]

[…]

At the which day it was agreid that for as moch as my lord the Mair for diwyrs causis Resonable must depairte owt of the Cite for a Certen tym that the Ryght Worshipfull Iohn Tong shalbe leutenaunt for my said lord the Mair to soch tym as he cumys ayan / And oer thys thys same day it was agreid that Iohn harper shall sell a vessill of white wyn that he has with owt ony thyng payng thar for to the Vinters pagent so that the said Iohn herper sell the said wyn for x d A galon //

York Prototype added note, [Liu] 2024: Leanne Groeneveld in her article “The York Vintners’ ‘The Marriage at Cana’ and the Puzzle of Pageants Withheld from the Register” identifies John Harper as a Merchant in York. John Tong was also a merchant and later mayor. Jenny Kermode, in her book Medieval Merchants: York, Beverley and Hull in the Later Middle Ages (Cambridge, 1998), thinks that John Haper and John Tong are related, as Harper was married to Tong’s stepdaughter Joan or Jane, daughter of Thomas Brounfleet.
